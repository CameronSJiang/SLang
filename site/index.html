Learn a language from the YouTube videos you already watch

SLang is a free Chrome extension. On YouTube, it shows the video’s subtitles in the language you are learning and writes a short translation above the words you probably don’t know yet. Click any word to hear it, see what it means, and save it for review. It can also dub English videos into Spanish with a voice that runs on your computer.

How it works

  1. Open any video with subtitles

    SLang turns on the video’s subtitles in the language you’re learning, or YouTube’s automatic ones if that’s all there is, and shows them in its own caption box. You can drag the box out of the way and change its size and style.

  2. Read along, look up what you need

    Words you probably don’t know get a short translation above them, at most two per line so the subtitles stay readable. Hover the caption to see the whole line in your own language. Click a word for its meaning, part of speech and pronunciation, and mark it as known, ignored or saved.

  3. Review what you saved

    Saved words keep the sentence and the moment in the video where you met them. A short quiz asks you about saved words and about words you have seen several times. Words you miss come back sooner, using spaced repetition.

Dubbing English videos

If a video is in English, SLang can translate its subtitles into Spanish and read them aloud in time with the video, while the original sound plays quietly underneath. Lines that run long are spoken a little faster, and the video waits briefly if a line still needs time.

The natural-sounding Spanish voice is a small speech model (Kokoro) that runs on your computer after a one-time download of about 90 MB. Other languages can be dubbed with the voices built into your operating system.

How SLang decides which words you know

Common words are usually learned first. SLang ranks words by how often they appear in film subtitles, books and websites, and starts from your level: either the one you pick, or the result of a three-minute vocabulary check (Spanish for now). From that, it estimates how likely you are to know each word.

The estimate changes as you watch. Looking up a word lowers it. Marking a word as known settles it. Seeing a word several times without looking it up raises it a little, and so does answering a quiz question about it correctly. Only words below the threshold get a translation hint, so the captions get quieter as you learn.

Privacy

Your word list, your progress and the subtitles of the videos you watch are stored in your browser and stay on your computer. SLang has no account, no analytics and no server of its own.

Translations come from the AI model built into Chrome, which also runs on your computer. If you prefer, you can add your own Anthropic API key instead; subtitles are then sent to that service to be translated.

Questions

What do I need?

Chrome on a Mac, Windows or Linux computer, and videos that have subtitles. Chrome’s built-in AI needs a one-time model download and a reasonably recent computer.

Does it cost anything?

SLang is free while it’s in beta. If we add a paid plan later, people on the waitlist will hear about it first.

Does it work on Netflix or on my phone?

Not yet. SLang works on YouTube in desktop Chrome. A phone version is the second most requested feature in our survey, and we’re looking into it.

What if a video has no subtitles in my language?

If it has English subtitles, SLang can translate them and dub the video. If it has no subtitles at all, SLang tells you so and stays out of the way.
