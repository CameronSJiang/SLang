01 — How it works

Watch normally. SLang handles the language part.

Five pieces that work together while a video plays. Pick one to see what it does.

Subtitles in the language you’re learning, with hints where you need them

SLang switches on the video’s own subtitles in your target language, or YouTube’s automatic ones, and draws them in its own caption box. Words you probably don’t know get a short translation above them, at most two per line. Click any word for its meaning, part of speech and pronunciation, then mark it known, ignored or saved.

  • hints per line≤ 2
  • hover a linefull translation
  • caption themes6

A model of what you know, updated as you watch

Every word has a frequency rank. SLang starts from your level and estimates how likely you are to know each word; only words below 50% get a hint. Looking a word up lowers its estimate, marking it known settles it, and seeing it a few times without looking it up raises it. Change the level to see the curve move.

English videos, read aloud in Spanish, in sync

SLang translates the English subtitles and reads each line in Spanish as it comes up, with the original sound lowered underneath. A line that runs long is spoken up to 1.6× faster; if it still needs time, the video waits a moment. The natural voice is Kokoro, an 82M-parameter speech model that runs on your computer’s graphics chip after a one-time download (about 325 MB; 90 MB on computers without a usable graphics chip, where it runs on the processor).

  • original audio while speaking15%
  • speech rate1.0–1.6×
  • lines prepared ahead4

Five-word quizzes that come back at the right time

Saved words keep the sentence and the moment in the video where you met them. Draco, SLang’s dragon, quizzes you on saved words and on words you’ve probably picked up. A miss saves the word and brings it back soon; a right answer pushes it further out. Scheduling uses FSRS, the algorithm behind modern flashcard apps.

  • questions per quiz5
  • keyboard1–4 answer · Enter next
  • schedulerFSRS

Your words and your viewing stay on your computer

The subtitles, your word list and your progress live in your browser’s storage. Translation runs on Chrome’s built-in AI model and the Spanish voice runs on Kokoro, both on your machine. SLang has no account, no analytics and no server. If you choose to add your own Anthropic API key, subtitles go to that service for translation, and only then.

04 — Privacy

Your words and your viewing stay yours.

SLang stores what it learns about you in your browser, on your computer. It doesn’t collect analytics and it doesn’t have a backend to send anything to. Uninstall it and the data goes with it.

In the free version, the only time anything leaves your machine is if you add your own Anthropic API key. Then the subtitles of the video you’re watching are sent to Anthropic to be translated. You can remove the key at any time.

Pro’s cloud translations will work the same way: the subtitles being translated go to our server and on to the AI model, and nothing else does. Your word list, quiz results and watch history stay on your computer. Pro needs an account only for billing. Read the full privacy policy.

05 — Questions

Before you sign up

SLang is in beta. The waitlist is a one-minute form; we email you when it’s on the Chrome Web Store.

Open the waitlist form
What do I need?

Chrome on a Mac, Windows or Linux computer, and videos with subtitles. Chrome’s built-in AI needs a one-time model download and a reasonably recent machine.

Does it cost anything?

No. Everything that runs on your computer is free. A Pro plan is planned for features that need servers, like cloud translations; it’s about $4 a month, with yearly, student and founding prices. The waitlist hears about it first.

Does it work on Netflix or my phone?

Not yet. SLang runs on YouTube in desktop Chrome. A phone version was the second most requested feature in our survey.

What if a video has no subtitles in my language?

If it has English subtitles, SLang can translate them and dub the video. With no subtitles at all, SLang says so and stays out of the way.

How is this different from dual subtitles?

Dual subtitles show a full translation all the time, so you read your own language. SLang shows the language you’re learning and only translates the few words you’re likely to miss, which keeps you reading in the new language.
